# Tax rules snapshot

**Snapshot date: October 2026. Tax year: 2026-27 (1 Apr 2026 – 31 Mar 2027).**
These figures are a starting point for verification, not an authority. Confirm each figure you use for the user's tax year and record it in the report's Rules Verification table.

## The 2026 transition

- The **Income-tax Act, 2025** applies from 1 April 2026, replacing the 1961 Act. "Previous year / assessment year" is replaced by a single **tax year**. Section numbers changed throughout (e.g. old 80C → new section 123, old 115BAC new regime → new section 202). Use the official section mapping on the Income Tax India site and cite **both** old and new numbers, since most users, Form 16s and articles still use the old ones.
- Budget 2026-27 made **no change to slabs or rates**.
- Income earned up to 31 March 2026 (FY 2025-26) is still assessed under the 1961 Act.

## What the new regime allows

Allowed: standard deduction, employer NPS contribution (old 80CCD(2), up to 14% of salary), interest on loan for a *let-out* property (but loss from house property cannot be set off against other income), family-pension deduction, Agniveer corpus.
Not allowed: 80C, 80D, HRA, LTA, 80CCD(1B), interest on self-occupied home loan, 80E, 80G, 80TTA/TTB, most allowances.

## Capital gains (resident individuals)

| Asset | Short-term if held | STCG | LTCG |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listed equity shares, equity MFs (STT paid) | ≤ 12 months | 20% | 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh exemption per year |
| Listed bonds, gold ETFs, listed REIT/InvIT units | ≤ 12 months | Slab | 12.5%, no indexation |
| Unlisted shares, physical gold, foreign shares (incl. RSUs), property | ≤ 24 months | Slab | 12.5%, no indexation |
| Debt MFs / "specified mutual funds" bought on or after 1 Apr 2023 | Any | Slab | Slab |
| Property bought before 23 Jul 2024 | — | — | Option: 12.5% without or 20% with indexation, for resident individuals/HUF |
| Virtual digital assets (crypto) | Any | 30% flat, no deductions except cost, no loss set-off; 1% TDS on transfers | Same |
| Sovereign Gold Bonds | — | — | Redemption at maturity tax-free **only** for bonds bought at original issue and held to maturity (Budget 2026). Secondary-market SGBs are taxed as capital gains. |

Other Budget 2026 changes affecting investors:
- **STT** from 1 Apr 2026: futures 0.05% (was 0.02%), options 0.15% (was 0.10%).
- **Share buybacks** are taxed as capital gains for shareholders (no longer as deemed dividend), with additional tax for promoters.
- **Interest expense** against dividend and mutual-fund income is no longer deductible.
- **TCS under LRS** for education and medical remittances above ₹10 lakh reduced to 2%; overseas tour packages 2% flat.
- A **foreign asset disclosure scheme** for small taxpayers was announced — verify whether it is open and its terms.

Set-off: short-term capital loss can offset STCG and LTCG; long-term loss only LTCG. Unabsorbed capital losses carry forward 8 years, only if the return is filed on time.

## Residency (verify under the new Act)

- **Resident** if in India ≥ 182 days in the tax year, or ≥ 60 days in the year and ≥ 365 days in the preceding four years. For Indian citizens leaving India for employment abroad, and for citizens/PIOs visiting, the 60-day leg is replaced by 182 days (120 days if Indian-source income exceeds ₹15 lakh).
- **Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR)**: e.g. non-resident in 9 of the previous 10 years, or ≤ 729 days in India in the previous 7 years; also deemed residents. RNORs are taxed broadly like non-residents on foreign income.
- A resident and ordinarily resident is taxed on worldwide income and must report foreign assets.
- DTAA relief and foreign tax credit (Form 67) where income is taxed abroad too.

## Other limits worth knowing

- DICGC deposit insurance: ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank (principal + interest).
- LRS remittance limit: USD 2,50,000 per year.
- PPF: ₹1.5 lakh per year; 15-year lock-in with partial withdrawal from year 7.
